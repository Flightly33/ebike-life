# E-Bike Life: project memory

A Roblox open-world e-bike game: ride Flightly e-bikes around a Miami-style city, earn money while riding (more for
wheelies, air time and tricks), and get chased by police for wheelies and stunts near cops. Bikes are
bought at the Flighty Mechanics dealership. It's a Rojo project in Luau, modelled on the owner's
reference videos (a Roblox e-scooter game): its HUD, menus, money loop and police chase.

## Working rules (from the owner)
- After every finished change, rebuild `EbikeLife.rbxl` (`rojo build default.project.json -o EbikeLife.rbxl`) and send the file to the user.
- Don't write README text.
- Develop and push on branch `claude/great-fermat-1koovo`. Don't open a PR unless asked.
- Push back when something isn't the best approach, and ask when an answer depends on details you don't have.
- Never invent Roblox asset or sound IDs. Sounds come from `AssetService:SearchAudioAsync` at runtime or from IDs the owner puts in `AssetIds`.
- Speeds are 1.5x real (`Config.SpeedBoost`, the owner's request): BikeStats scales every cut-off by it, power by its cube and torque by it; the battery drain divides by its cube so range in minutes stays put.
- Bikes are real light e-moto types (Sur-Ron class: throttle, no pedals, mid-motor and chain) with real-world specs (peak power, pack Wh and volts, weight, tyres). The owner asked for no pedal bikes.

## Open issues (not yet confirmed in Studio)
Everything below passes the simulator, but nothing has been played in Studio yet.
1. The bike is kinematic and client-owned: the client runs `BikePhysics.Step` every frame and writes the root CFrame and velocity (an anti-gravity VectorForce holds it up). Replication to other players, network ownership hand-off on mount, and the seat weld need checking.
2. Rider pose is procedural (`src/client/RiderPose.luau`, maths in `RiderIK.luau`, joints via `Rig.luau`): every client writes every rider's joint Transforms in `RunService.PreSimulation` (after Roblox's animation step), sets the SeatWeld locally so the hips sit on the seat, leans the torso until the hands reach the grips, and solves two-bone IK to `GripL/R` and `PedalL/R` / `PegL/R`. On the gas the rider leans forward, wheelies pull back, tricks have full-body poses. The bike root has `RootPriority` 100 so its frame is what replicates. Unverified in Studio.
3. Police and traffic cars are server-owned, unanchored, driven kinematically by `CarDriver` along the road graph. Check they don't jitter or sink.
4. Audio search needs "Allow HTTP / Studio API access" and may return nothing in an unpublished place; the game falls back to `rbxasset://` sounds.
5. DataStores need Studio API access; without it DataService runs from an in-memory profile.
6. Game pass IDs in `AssetIds.GamePasses` are 0 (passes disabled).
7. Bails ragdoll the rider on the client (`src/client/Ragdoll.luau`: BallSocketConstraints over Motor6D or AnimationConstraint joints, the bike's exact velocity, only body parts collide, NoCollisionConstraints across each joint, the SeatWeld cut locally), then they get up where they landed (the GotUp remote parks the bike where it slid to) and walk back to get on; the server keeps the ride for `BAIL_GRACE` while they ragdoll. Unverified in Studio.
8. On-foot officers (`src/server/Officer.luau`, part-built R6 rigs in `Workspace.Officers`) walk with `Humanoid:MoveTo`; check they path around cars and kerbs.
9. Suspension visuals: the local bike draws its own suspension state; other players' bikes raycast each wheel's ground from their replicated frame (`Visuals.measureSuspension`). Check both look right, and that wheels don't sink or float.
10. Miami map: Biscayne Bay and the ocean are Terrain water that `CityService` fills at runtime (not in the baked model, so Studio's edit view shows the bay floor); check it fills and that riding or jumping into it splashes and puts the bike back on the shore. Towers set `Model.LevelOfDetail = StreamingMesh` so the skyline shows past the streaming radius; check it does. AI cars on the freeway ramps, the I-95/I-395 junction and the bridge are unverified.
If any of these fail in Studio, ask the user for the Output window's errors and warnings.

## Layout

- `src/server`: DataService (DataStore, attributes Cash/Level/Xp), BikeService (spawn/park/mount, validates BikeState), EconomyService (pay loop, tricks, combos, levels), ShopService (dealership, upgrades, paints, crates, boosts, teleports, modes), PoliceService (sight, heat, dispatch, bust, escape; roles Lead/Flank/Intercept/Roadblock, no PIT or ramming (police don't use contact on motorcycles): cars tail at `FollowGap` and pace at `FlankOffset`, and only close in once the rider is under `BoxInKmh`; spike strips flatten tyres, officers deploy on foot and the bust meter fills only with an officer in `ArrestRange` of a helpless rider), Officer (on-foot police), RiderLook, TrafficService, CarDriver (car AI, `Hold` for roadblocks), CityService (builds the city if the baked map is missing, fills the bay with Terrain water, charger prompts), CharacterService, AudioService.
- `src/client`: BikeController (local ride, events -> Trick remote; bail/ragdoll/remount), Ragdoll, FX (drift smoke, skids, sparks, landing dust), BikeCamera, Input (keyboard, gamepad, touch), Visuals (wheels, cranks, motor/siren sound, police lights, traffic lights), RiderPose (procedural rider pose), RiderIK, Rig, World (ray/blockcast adapter), Sound, Music, State, UI/ (Hud, Speedometer, Minimap, TrickHud, WantedHud, Toasts, Rides (My Rides + dealership), Tuning, Store, MainMenu, TouchControls).
- `map/City.rbxm`: baked city (~118k parts; Workspace streaming is on, target radius 1536 (min 384; the owner found the view too short), vehicles are Atomic models). `tools/lune/preview_scene.luau` renders street spots with an avatar-sized dummy, a bike and a car for scale checks, freeway, bridge, beach and skyline views and a top-down map (with a stand-in water plane).
- `map/City.rbxm`, rebaking: Rebake with `lune run tools/lune/bake_city.luau` after any change to City/ or the bike and car models (the dealership and police station hold static copies).
- `tools/preview`: offline three.js renders of the map and models (`node render.mjs`, Playwright Chromium).
- `blender/`: `build_vehicles.py` (with `meshkit.py`) models every bike and car in Blender and writes `blender/out/Vehicles.fbx` and `src/shared/Models/MeshManifest.luau`. Run `lune run tools/lune/export_vehicles.luau` first (rig points from BikeModel.Geometry, car dims), then `/root/tools/blendenv/bin/python blender/build_vehicles.py [--render all|id,id] [--only id,id]`. bpy 5.2 (pip, Python 3.13 venv at /root/tools/blendenv), Cycles on CPU. Meshes are split as `<Group>__<Slot>` (group = the joint it rides on, slot = colour/material, MeshKit.Slots).
- E-motos are modelled in `blender/emoto.py`, each on a real family (`FAMILY` by bike id) so the line-up looks different: Bee (Sur-Ron Light Bee: raw silver spars, slim covers), Sting (Talaria Sting: black frame, big vented side panels), Storm (Sur-Ron Storm/Ultra Bee: louvred MX shrouds, side number boards), Box (Segway X160/X260: exposed ribbed battery box), Cafe (scrambler: teardrop tank, brown bench, cast wheels on the street bikes). The side stand pivots on a lug outboard of the chain line. The chain runs (`ChainTop` / `ChainBot`) and the front `Sprocket` are their own mesh groups on `ChainTopSlide` / `ChainBotSlide` / `SprocketSpin` joints; `Visuals.driveChain` slides each run by the rear sprocket radius times the wheel angle (wrapped to one link) and spins the sprocket 48:14. Common parts (Light Bee class: forged twin-spar frame, side covers, shrouds, USD fork in triple clamps, 4-piston calipers on wavy rotors, 36-spoke wheels on knobby or road tyres, box swingarm, piggyback shock, finned motor, controller, MX pegs, switchgear). City kit: `blender/citykit.py` builds `Kit_Window`, `Kit_Tree_Broadleaf/Birch/Conifer`, `Kit_Lamp`, `Kit_Bench`, `Kit_Hydrant`, `Kit_Bin`, `Kit_Bollard` and `Kit_Meter` into the same Vehicles.fbx; `src/shared/Models/CityKit.luau` (run by CityService on the server) swaps them in for `WindowFrame`+`Sill`, `Tree` models (by `Species`), `Lamp`, `Bench` and `Hydrant` models and loose `Bin`, `Bollard` and `MeterPole` posts (fitted to boxes it measures itself: a model pivot here is a rotated cylinder), keeping the glass, globes, trunks and poles (colliders, night lights). Kit meshes stay lean (window ~280 tris, tree ~2.5k, lamp ~1.3k).
- The owner imports Vehicles.fbx in Studio (File > Import 3D) into ReplicatedStorage as `VehicleMeshes`. MeshKit swaps those in for the part-built visuals (joints, seat, attachments, lamps, signs stay) and falls back to parts when they're missing or imported rotated. The rbxl built here does not contain them (they need uploading from Studio).
- Suspension (`src/shared/Suspension.luau`, run by `BikePhysics.Step` every frame): a planar 4-DOF spring-damper model (sprung chassis height and pitch, each axle's height) integrated in 1/960 s substeps. Progressive springs with preload set from each bike's real travel (`Geometry.FrontTravel` / `RearTravel`) at 30% rider sag, compression and rebound damping, bump and top-out stops, one-sided tyre springs, weight transfer (squat, dive), hop preload, landings at 0.6 of the vertical speed (the legs take the rest). On the ground it works in world heights: `BikePhysics.wheelSupport` rays 5 points across each tyre's footprint so a wheel rolls up a kerb edge (enveloped over 0.6 R), and `Suspension.Roughness` adds deterministic terrain bumps per surface (grass, dirt, mud, sand bumpy; paving and roads nearly smooth). Loops run it in the loop's frame. `BikePhysics.GetCFrame` draws the chassis at the suspension's height and pitch; `BikeModel` is drawn topped out and `BikeModel.SuspensionPose` gives the joint Transforms (fork lowers slide up the steering axis on `ForkSlide`, the swingarm turns on `SwingJoint`, the shock's body and shaft on `ShockJoint` / `ShockRodJoint` stay in line). E-moto forks are long enough for the tyre to clear the lower crown at full travel, capped so the bars stay at `BarHeight`. Blender meshes ride the same groups (`Fork`, `Swing`, `ShockTop`, `ShockEnd`).
- Turning (`Config.Turning`) copies the owner's reference scooter game: above walking pace the bars set a turn rate (~85 degrees a second at 60 km/h), not tyre grip; the lean is half the physical lean, capped at 34 degrees. The bars ease toward the input (`SteerRate`, slower at speed) and the bike rolls into and out of a turn on a critically damped spring (`RollIn`, `State.TurnRate`), so turns build up over ~0.3 s instead of snapping (the owner found it punchy). Surfaces steeper than `Physics.MaxRidePitch` launch you along them, and a quarter-pipe launch turns you round at the top (`Fakie`). Ramp faces (hit normal Y > 0.3) never count as walls, and neither does any part of a ramp, loop or hill: `World.Sweep` reports tilted parts and natural ground (grass, dirt, sand, mud) as "Slope", which the bike rides through (the ground rays carry it). The collision box tilts with the ground (`BikePhysics.Body`). Drift (R) locks the rear (no drive); a held slide tapers toward `Drift.HoldSlip` and never reaches `SpinOut`.
- Ramp physics (found with `tools/lune/ride_ramps.luau`, see Checks): the ground rays start `half·tan(MaxRidePitch)` above each tyre so steep faces are found; the pitch follows down slopes too (only a real lip, or a ramp's end face just under its lip, holds it) and changes no faster than the tyres roll (`StepClimb`); the airborne test predicts the rear's rise from the ground actually covered (no takeoff/land flicker on concave faces); dropping in, the rear follows the front's line; a face steeper than `MaxRidePitch` only launches you if you were already climbing it (`VertEntry`), otherwise it's a wall; quarter pipes launch straight up (`VertCarry` 0) and the fakie drifts back over the face; landings keep the speed along the slope and the second wheel comes down over `Settle` (`FrontDropRate` / `RearDropRate`); you can't land on a face too steep to ride (you slide off it) or on ground above where the bike was (a step's face). Kicker back boards sit under the lip; the quarter pipe coping is `Surface = Ramp`.
- Wheelies are balanced, not parked: above `Wheelie.Balance` gravity tips the bike further (`Unstable`), so holding the key and throttle loops out in ~2 s; riders keep it up by letting off the throttle and dabbing the rear brake (the owner found key-hold too easy).
- Tricks: air tricks (Z Superman, X Can-Can, T Nac-Nac, G Knee Knocker, N No-Footer; Y barspin), stoppie (brake+Shift), drift (R), loops (`LoopEntry` plates with `LoopRadius`/`LoopShift`, path-followed by `BikePhysics` via `World.Loop`), foam pits always land. Paid by EconomyService (air-validated, whitelisted, capped per jump). The rider tucks in on their own before touchdown, so a held key never bails. The HUD shows the tricks numbered in that order above the key hints, lighting up the one being done. `tools/lune/reach.luau` prints seat / pedal / grip distances per bike. The Jump blocks are (0,3), the Jump Park, and (1,3), the Loop Park (`Landmarks.LoopPark`).
- Realism: `Shared/Realism` gives parts real materials by role (rubber tyres, shingle / clay-tile house roofs, leather seats, reflective glass, glossy vehicle paint) and runs in Builder, CarModel and BikeModel; MaterialService `Use2022Materials` is on; `AssetIds.Materials` takes the owner's own PBR texture sets (Shared/Materials makes MaterialVariants that override the base materials at startup; never invent ids). The DayNight looks are graded like a photo (natural saturation, soft contrast, dark desaturated nights).
- Day and night: `Shared/DayNight` runs a 20-minute day off the server time (`Config.DayNight`; daylight 06-20 takes 70%) with keyframed looks (lighting, atmosphere, bloom, grading, sun rays); `Client/DayNight` applies them locally, lights lamp `Globe`s (real PointLights only on the nearest `MaxLampLights`), lights a stable share of `Window`/`ShopGlass` parts warm at night, and puts SpotLights on headlights (your bike, nearby bikes, the nearest cars). The admin time command sets ReplicatedStorage's `ClockOffset`.
- Body cam: first person (F / R3, `BikeCamera.FirstPerson`) is the rider's eyes (head and accessories hidden) tilted 0.3 rad down so the hands, forearms, bars and front wheel show; its FOV is the player's setting (`Settings.Fov`, 60-120, default 90; Menus > Settings slider). Menus > Tricks lists every trick and control (keyboard and controller).
- The Miami map (`City/Layout`, north is -Z): a 9x9 mainland grid 360 apart (avenues Miami Ave x = 0 and Flagler St z = 0, Biscayne Blvd x = 1440 along the bay; `Layout.X_NAMES` / `Z_NAMES`), districts by block in `DISTRICTS` (row = z): glass condo towers on parking podiums with pool decks (Edgewater, Downtown, Brickell; `Buildings.CondoTower` / `Podium`, offices `Buildings.Tower`), Wynwood mural warehouses (`Buildings.Mural`), pastel Midtown, made-up fast-food chains with drive-thrus and pylons (`Buildings.Brands`: Burger Bay, Cluck Shack, Taco Ola...; never real trademarks), strip malls, gas stations, car parks and a ridable four-deck garage (`Buildings.Garage`, ramps alternate lanes, all climbing north), the suburbs (stucco houses with clay roofs and pools), Bayfront Park, the marina, and the kept landmarks (Flighty Mechanics, Police, the Plaza, the Jump and Loop parks). Freeways (`City/Freeways`) on piers `FREEWAY_Y` up: I-95 down block column 4 (descending to the turnpike at both ends), on/off ramps in rows 1 and 5 (`Layout.Ramps`: Foot on the street, Base, Top, Join; merge plates beside the deck), I-395 east from the junction over Biscayne Blvd and across the bay as a bridge onto the island's 5th St. The turnpike is a U (north, west, south) whose ends meet Biscayne Blvd. East of `SHORE_X` is water (`Layout.Water`, `InWater`): the bay, the beach island (West Ave, Collins Ave, Ocean Dr; condo towers and art-deco hotels, `Buildings.DecoHotel`), the beach sloping into the surf, and two causeways at z = +-900 (`City/Shore`: seawall, baywalk, revetments, causeways, docks and boats). ## Checks (run before every push)
- `tools/tc.sh`: Luau strict type check (luau-lsp, old solver). Empty output means clean.
- `lune run tests/physics.luau`: ride model checks (top speeds per mode and class, range, wheelie balance, jumps, landings, hops, surfaces, and the suspension: sag, kerbs up and down, dive, squat, landings within the bump stops, grass vs road, wheelies, loops, frame hitches, every bike settling).
- `lune run tools/lune/ride_ramps.luau <bikeId> 25,45,70 [filter] [verbose] [trace=t0-t1]`: rides BikePhysics over every ramp, kicker, quarter pipe and hill side in the baked city (real part geometry via `tools/lune/geoworld.luau`), up each face at those speeds and down each from the top; exits 1 on a takeoff/land flicker or a pitch snap. It covers the freeway ramps, descents and bridge and the garage ramps too, with the bay's water. Crashes it lists are straight-line runs into buildings and barriers. Run it for flit_mini, storm_x and halo_gt after physics or ramp changes.
- `lune run tests/consistency.luau`: data checks (bikes, paints, upgrades, modes, layout: graph connected, nothing driven on in the water but bridges and causeways, climbs only on freeways and ramps; and in the baked map every place and charger lands on open ground).
- `lune run tests/pose.luau`: rider pose maths (limbs reach their targets, knees and elbows bend the right way, lean direction) on a standard and a rotated-attachment R15 body.
- `lune run tests/meshes.luau`: the Blender mesh path with a faked import (every vehicle, joints, fallback), and the suspension joints on the part-built bikes (each wheel rises by its compression, the shock stays in line, the fork lowers clear the crown).
- `rojo sourcemap default.project.json -o sourcemap.json && lune run tests/sim_smoke.luau`: boots server and client in the simulator (`tools/sim`, virtual clock) and plays a session: buy, ride, modes, wheelies, tricks, payouts, police chase, bust, escape. The one expected warning is the runtime city build.
- Tools: Lune 0.11, Rojo 7.7; luau-lsp at /root/tools/luau-lsp.
- The simulator only approximates Roblox. A passing sim doesn't prove the game works in Studio.

## Conventions
- Scale: one scale for everything, 1 m = 2.86 studs (`Config.STUDS_PER_METER`, 1 stud = 0.35 m), matched to Roblox avatars (~5.2 studs tall). Physics runs in SI and converts at the edges. The city is authored in Roblox's standard units (1 stud = 0.28 m, real sizes) via `Layout.Design`, and `Builder` scales it by `Config.WORLD_SCALE` (1.25); the runtime `Layout` is already scaled. Each spawned bike is drawn to fit its rider: `BikeService` measures the character's hip-to-ankle length and `BikeModel.FitScale` picks the size where the knees bend to ~85% on the pegs (a default avatar: 0.87-1.17x across the line-up; bigger avatars get proportionally bigger bikes). The model carries it as the `Scale` attribute, `BikeModel.Build({ Scale })` draws at `VISUAL_STUDS_PER_METER * Scale`, and `BikeStats.Scaled` grows the spec's wheels and wheelbase to match (power, mass, travel and speeds stay real). Showroom and menu bikes are drawn at 1. Wheels are placed on the ground actually under them each frame (`Visuals.measureSuspension`, the local bike too). Physics speeds stay in real units. Anything true-size placed inside the city build (showroom bikes, parked cars) is pre-scaled by 1/WORLD_SCALE.
- Server time (`workspace:GetServerTimeNow()`) for traffic lights and anything shared; `os.clock()` for local timing.
- The server never trusts client money: the client reports events (land, flip, wheelie end), the server checks them against the bike's state and pays.
- UI is built from Instances only (no image IDs): ViewportFrames for 3D icons, emoji glyphs, Frames for the 7-segment digits.
- Commit messages end with the Co-Authored-By and Claude-Session lines the session provides.
